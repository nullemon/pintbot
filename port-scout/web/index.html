A LITTLE GUIDANCE

From home network to travel-ready.

1. Find your camera’s local IP

Check your router’s connected-device list or the camera’s app. Add a single IP such as 192.168.1.100, a range such as 192.168.1.100-120, or a subnet such as 192.168.1.0/24. CIDR excludes IPv4 network and broadcast addresses where applicable. Duplicate IPs are merged.

For IPv6, enter a full address or a small CIDR range. Scoped link-local addresses use the interface suffix required by your operating system, if supported by the scanner.

2. Review, then scan

All TCP ports checks ports 1–65,535. Camera ports checks a smaller set, including common web interfaces and RTSP. No ping response is required before scanning. Bulk subnets can take a while; try camera ports first when you are locating a device.

“Open” means a TCP connection succeeded. Closed means it was refused. No response can mean filtering, an offline device, or a short timeout. Errors can indicate an unreachable network or local resource limits. An open port alone does not prove a device is a camera, and services can use unusual ports. Use “Check service” on any open port to inspect RTSP or HTTP/HTTPS. “Video confirmed” means video data was received; “Video advertised” means the service described a stream without delivering video. Port numbers, including 8000, never prove that a camera is present. UDP discovery and in-browser RTSP playback are not provided.

3. Connect while traveling

A private IP such as 192.168.x.x works on your home network, or through a VPN into that network. Use your router’s supported VPN or a trusted VPN setup to reach your camera when away. Scanning your public IP from home may show router behavior rather than the view from outside.

A successful scan does not enable remote access or verify camera credentials. Avoid exposing camera administration and streaming ports directly to the internet. Check the camera manufacturer’s instructions for secure remote access.

Getting reliable results

Start with the default settings. For older cameras, reduce connections to 32. For Wi-Fi, VPN, or distant devices, raise the timeout to 1,500–3,000 ms. Retry a scan when a device was asleep or temporarily busy. CSV exports open ports; JSON also includes per-host summaries, settings, and service verification results. Use your camera’s actual stream path and optional account to verify services that need authentication.

A session supports up to 4,096 targets and 20,000,000 port checks. Results remain in memory until the scanner is restarted or replaced by a new scan. Export anything you want to keep.
